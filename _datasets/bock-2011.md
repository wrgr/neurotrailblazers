Bock and colleagues recorded the preferred stimulus orientation of neurons in mouse primary
visual cortex by two-photon calcium imaging, then imaged the same tissue by serial-section
TEM. The aligned EM volume spans about 450 × 350 × 52 μm and holds about 1,500 cell bodies.
Fourteen of them were the functionally characterized cells, and the team traced part of their
local network. The main result: inhibitory interneurons received convergent input from nearby
excitatory cells with a broad range of preferred orientations. MICrONS later scaled the same
structure-and-function design to a cubic millimeter.

**What it is good for.** Seeing where the co-registration problem starts. Matching a cell in a
calcium movie to the same cell in EM is still the hard part of every functional connectomics
experiment.

**What it does not support.** Dense connectivity analysis. The team traced a small part of
the volume by hand, starting from 14 cells, and the paper notes that weak orientation biases
in the inhibitory input could not be ruled out.
