This is the cleanest early demonstration that a wiring asymmetry explains a computation.
Direction-selective ganglion cells were identified by calcium imaging, then the same retina
was imaged by serial block-face SEM and the starburst amacrine inputs traced. The wiring was
asymmetric in the way the direction preference predicted.

Do not confuse it with Kim et al. (2014), the EyeWire paper, which is about a different
synapse in the same circuit: bipolar cells onto starburst amacrine cells.

**What it is good for.** The standard worked example of a structural claim earning its
functional interpretation, which is the standard the site's rubrics point at.

**What it does not support.** Generalization beyond this circuit. The retina is unusually
tractable: a thin, layered, well-characterized structure with a known computation to explain.
