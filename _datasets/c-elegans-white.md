White and colleagues reconstructed the nervous system of the nematode by hand from
serial-section electron micrographs, a project that ran from 1969 to the mid-1980s: 302 neurons, about 5,000 chemical synapses, 2,000
neuromuscular junctions and 600 gap junctions. The whole wiring diagram fits in a
spreadsheet. The images on BossDB are the N2U series, printed from glass-plate negatives,
digitized in David Hall's lab, and aligned later by Chris Brittin and Steve Cook.

**What it is good for.** Understanding what a connectome is, without any of the scale
problems. Every graph-analysis technique the site teaches can be rehearsed here in seconds,
and because the worm's cell lineage is nearly invariant, every neuron has a known identity
and a name.

**What it does not support.** The assumption that structure determines behavior. The worm
wiring diagram has been published for forty years and much of its dynamics is still
unexplained, because neuromodulation and extrasynaptic signaling are invisible to EM. It is
also a composite: the 1986 diagram was assembled from sections of more than one animal, so it
says nothing about variation between individuals. For that, see Witvliet et al. (2021).
