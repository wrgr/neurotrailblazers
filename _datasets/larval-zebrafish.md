Hildebrand and colleagues imaged the whole brain of a 5.5-day-old larval zebrafish by
serial-section EM. To keep acquisition time and data volume manageable, they imaged in
several rounds at different resolutions, targeting the regions that needed detail. From the
data they reconstructed all myelinated axons (the projectome) and found striking bilateral
symmetry in reticulospinal and lateral line afferent axons. Functional reference atlases and
in vivo two-photon data from the same fish were co-registered to the volume.

**What it is good for.** The vertebrate case. Almost everything else at EM resolution is an
invertebrate or a fragment of a mammal. This is a whole vertebrate brain, small enough to fit
in one volume and transparent enough to have been imaged alive first.

**What it does not support.** Dense synaptic connectivity. This is a projectome, myelinated
axons traced across the brain, not a reconstruction of every synapse. Treating it as a
connectome in the FlyWire sense misreads what was measured. The multi-scale imaging also means
resolution is not uniform across the volume.
