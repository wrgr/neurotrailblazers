Most connectomics datasets are one species, one animal, one time point. This one samples
several ages in each of two species, mouse (from postnatal day 6 to day 523) and rhesus
macaque (from day 7 to day 3,000), and counts excitatory and inhibitory synapses onto
excitatory neurons. The finding is in the title: synapses form, and mostly prune, on a
similar absolute schedule in both species, despite their very different lifespans. Mouse
inhibitory synapses are the exception and keep being added throughout life.

**What it is good for.** The comparative question the field mostly cannot yet ask. Use it when
a learner assumes a mouse result transfers to a primate.

**What it does not support.** Whole-circuit analysis. The sampling is designed for synapse
counts at each age, not for connectivity, and the mice in the study were all male.
