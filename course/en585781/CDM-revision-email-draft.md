Hi all,

I have rebuilt the slides for my three modules (7–9, the connectomics block) and in
doing so found a handful of CDM changes worth making. Nothing structural — the
assessment mix, the credit weighting, and the test schedule are all unchanged. I have
attached a short document with the proposed text; this note is the summary.

**Three things prompted the revision.**

First, Melissa's note on the CDM about avoiding double-barrelled learning outcomes. Six
of my nine MLOs currently carry two verbs. I have rewritten them one-verb-per-outcome,
which takes the block from nine outcomes to twelve. They are more specific and, more to
the point, each is now gradable against something a student actually hands in.

Second, the field moved. FlyWire's whole adult fly brain, the MICrONS flagship release,
connectome-constrained models that predict neural activity, and a light-microscopy route
to dense synapse-level reconstruction all landed after the previous slides were written.
The module topics should reflect that, and the decks now do.

Third, the three modules now share one spine — a single discovery pipeline running from
question to specimen to image to reconstruction to graph to claim. Module 7 owns the two
ends, Module 8 the middle, Module 9 the conversion of measurements into defensible
claims. Making that explicit in the CDM is what turns the block from three topics into
one cumulative argument.

**One change is worth flagging because it removes content.** Macroscale connectomics —
diffusion MRI, and X-ray microtomography alongside it — now appears once, as the example
that different questions need different instruments, rather than as a topic to be
covered. Two reasons: modules 10–12 already cover human non-invasive methods in real
depth, and the connectomics block earns its place by teaching what only
synapse-resolution structure can establish. Naming the boundary is useful; re-teaching
the material is duplicative. Happy to be argued out of this one if the sequencing looks
different from where you sit.

**Also unchanged but now named:** each module gets one explicit graded artifact — a
one-page study brief for Module 7, a reproducible query against a public dataset for
Module 8, and an analysis card with a stated null model for Module 9. These are what the
short-answer questions were already circling; naming them makes the rubric easier to
write and the expectations clearer to students.

**Two small things outside my modules,** noticed while working through the document
rather than sought out:

- Brock's comments ask whether the module order should be re-established as a preferred
  sequence. From my side: 7–9 are internally sequential and need to stay in that order
  relative to each other, but the block does not depend on modules 1–6 and can sit
  anywhere after the introduction.
- Module 16's learning objectives are numbered 14.1–14.3, duplicating module 14. Looks
  like a copy-paste artifact and a one-line fix.

The rebuilt decks are done — 58, 55, and 57 slides respectively, each in three parts,
with speaker notes — and they are written to the proposed outcomes. If the outcomes
change in review I will update the decks to match rather than the other way round, so
there is no cost to pushing back.

Kelly, Melissa — happy to walk through any of this on a call if that is faster than
comments on the document.

Thanks,
Will
