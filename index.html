This site turns them into something a person can work through: an ordered course for students, a reference layer that states what connectomics does not yet support, and session kits so a mentor or program can teach from it without building the material first. The Story, in One Minute

Why this site exists, before you choose where to go in it.

Brains compute through wiring: billions of cells connected by trillions of synapses. For most of history that wiring was invisible. Electron microscopes can now photograph every wire in a piece of brain, and Nature Methods named EM-based connectomics its Method of the Year for 2025. Complete maps exist for a worm and an adult fly brain, and about a cubic millimeter each of mouse and human cortex has been reconstructed. The next target is a whole mouse brain. Getting there is a relay with seven legs, and each part of this site teaches one of them:

  1. Stop time. Chemicals freeze the tissue mid-thought; heavy-metal stains ink every wire's outline.
  2. Slice. A diamond knife shaves it thousands of times thinner than a hair.
  3. Image. Electron microscopes photograph every slice — about two million gigabytes per cubic millimeter.
  4. Align. Software reassembles the slices into one continuous 3D volume.
  5. Segment. Machine-learning models trace each neuron and flag its synapses, and they make mistakes.
  6. Proofread. People find and fix those mistakes, where the science needs it most.
  7. Discover. Scientists query the map to test ideas about how circuits compute, and state what wiring alone cannot show.
