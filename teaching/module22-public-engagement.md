This is a **companion session** for [Module 22]({{ '/modules/module22/' | relative_url }}),
Scientific Writing and Presentation. It does not replace the module or its
[session kit]({{ '/teaching/sessions/module22/' | relative_url }}).

Module 22 teaches one rule for every audience:

> adapt vocabulary, analogy, and background depth; never the evidentiary standard.

This companion applies that rule to public engagement: school groups, public programs
and other audiences outside research.

## Learning objectives

Learners will be able to:

1. explain atlas, projectome, cellular connectome, synaptic connectome and activity map;
2. explain why IC3 and APEX have complementary roles;
3. use at least one authentic scientific resource rather than a simulated replacement;
4. distinguish program intent, scientific result and demonstrated impact;
5. communicate one limitation without jargon;
6. pose one meaningful public or neuroethics question.

## 60-minute session

### 0–10 min: one brain, many maps

Use:

- [Unit 02, Brain Data Across Scales]({{ '/technical-training/02-brain-data-across-scales/' | relative_url }})
  for the atlas, projectome and connectome distinction;
- the connectivity ladder from
  [From Projectome to Synapse]({{ '/teaching/projectome-to-synapse/' | relative_url }})
  (about 10 minutes). The full activity takes 25–40 minutes and can run as its own session.

### 10–25 min: authentic resource lab

Choose one:

- the Allen Brain Cell (ABC) Atlas, using Allen's own beginner guide;
- [FlyWire Codex](https://codex.flywire.ai/) as an external connectomics precedent;
- APEX or CONNECTS resources, once they are publicly usable;
- IC3 or CONNECTS Knowledge Base resources, once they are publicly usable.

**Rule:** NeuroTrailblazers provides the learning task, not a duplicate browser manual.

### 25–40 min: follow the evidence

Use:

- [How we fit BRAIN CONNECTS]({{ '/core/connects-ecosystem/' | relative_url }}) and
  [Major Initiatives]({{ '/initiatives/' | relative_url }}) to trace a program's intent
  and capabilities;
- [Global Outreach &amp; Citizen Science]({{ '/initiatives/outreach/' | relative_url }})
  for adoption and public benefit;
- [NIH RePORTER](https://reporter.nih.gov/) for the award record behind any BRAIN
  CONNECTS claim.

Learners work in pairs, each pair taking one program, and trace this chain:

**intent → capability → artifact → adoption → scientific consequence → translation or public benefit**

Stop at the last step the evidence supports.

### 40–50 min: public translation

Each learner produces:

- one 60-second explanation;
- one evidence badge: Established, Emerging or Aspirational;
- one sentence beginning “This evidence does not yet show…”

### 50–60 min: ethics and public voice

Prompt:

> As brain maps become more detailed, what should researchers, participants, communities
> and policymakers be able to decide together?

## Assessment

A passing artifact includes:

- one authentic resource;
- one explicit scale label;
- one source or provenance statement;
- one bounded scientific claim;
- one limitation;
- one audience-appropriate “so what?”;
- one ethics or public-engagement question.
