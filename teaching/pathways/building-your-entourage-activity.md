**Time:** 20 minutes in pairs, 15 minutes in pairs, then 15 minutes alone. The case
below is **invented**. Leila, Dr. Brandt and everyone else named are not real people.

## The case

Leila is a fourth-year student in a connectomics lab. Dr. Brandt, her PI, has just
announced a move to another university next year. Leila realizes that most of her
professional contacts came through Dr. Brandt. Here is everyone she can think of:

1. **Dr. Mensah**, a collaborator at another university. Leila's analysis is half of
   their joint paper. Every email goes through Dr. Brandt, with Leila copied.
2. **Jae**, a postdoc in another lab. At Leila's poster three months ago, Jae said:
   "Send me an email about your null model." Leila has not.
3. **The consortium proofreading channel.** Leila reads it daily. She has never posted.
   She asks her own questions by direct message to one labmate.
4. **Farah**, a former lab member now at a company doing imaging software. They
   overlapped for a year. They have not spoken since Farah left.
5. **Owen**, a staff scientist in the imaging core. They talk weekly, only about data.
6. **Ben**, a student in another department. Leila helps him with code every Thursday.
   She has never asked him for anything.

## 1. Map the ties (20 minutes)

For each of the six, write:

- **Origin:** **B** if the tie came through Dr. Brandt and still runs through him;
  **O** if Leila started it or it now runs directly.
- **Flows each way:** what Leila gives, and what she gets.
- **Role they could fill**, from the [Module 24]({{ '/modules/module24/' | relative_url }})
  mentorship map: technical supervisor, sponsor, near-peer, field connector, or the
  person with no stake in whether she stays.
- **Survives the move?** Yes, no, or only if Leila acts.

Then answer: which roles are empty? Which single tie is cheapest to make Leila's own?

## 2. Three first messages (15 minutes)

Write three messages for Leila, five sentences or fewer each:

1. To **Jae**, three months late.
2. A **first post** in the consortium channel: an answer, a small contribution, or a
   question asked in public instead of by direct message.
3. To **Dr. Mensah** or **Farah**, directly.

Each message must name something specific to that person, ask one question they can
answer in a sentence, and offer something back. For the Dr. Mensah message, write one
line on what Leila tells Dr. Brandt first.

Then write one sentence Leila could say to Ben that keeps helping him but makes the
tie run both ways.

## 3. Your own map (15 minutes, private)

This section is yours. You do not have to share it, and you will not send anything
during the session.

1. List up to ten people in your professional life. Initials are fine.
2. Mark each **B** or **O**, and write what flows each way in a few words.
3. Mark the Module 24 role each fills. Circle the roles nobody fills.
4. Mark each tie that would not survive your main mentor leaving.
5. Draft three first messages: one **reconnect** to a dormant tie, one **give-first**
   message that offers something without an ask, and one **specific question** to
   someone outside your group.

## Peer check and exit ticket

Swap *one* section 2 message with your partner. Your partner answers one question:
*could this have been sent to anyone else?* If yes, rewrite the first sentence.

Exit ticket: "The message I will send first is to ___ (initials). I will send it by ___ (date)."

**Follow-through this week:** send at least one of your three messages. Record the
reply or the silence. Keep your map; a later workshop asks for it.

**Success criteria:** each tie is sorted by origin and by what flows each way; empty
roles are named; every message is specific to its reader, asks one answerable
question and offers something back; your map marks which ties are borrowed.
