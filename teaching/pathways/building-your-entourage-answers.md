These responses use the worksheet's **invented** case. They are examples, not the only
acceptable wording. This page is public and suitable for formative feedback.

## 1. Map the ties

| Person | Origin | Flows each way | Role | Survives the move? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dr. Mensah | B | Leila gives analysis; gets co-authorship, via Brandt. | Sponsor or field connector | Only if Leila writes directly. |
| Jae | O, fading | Nothing yet; Jae offered a conversation. | Field connector | Only if Leila acts soon. |
| Channel | Neither | Leila takes answers; gives nothing visible. | Near-peers, field connectors | Yes, but she is invisible in it. |
| Farah | B, dormant | Nothing since Farah left. | Near-peer for industry routes | Only if Leila acts. |
| Owen | O | Data questions both ways. | Person with no stake | Yes. |
| Ben | O | Leila gives weekly hours; gets nothing. | Near-peer | Yes, but one-way. |

**Empty roles:** sponsor. Only Dr. Brandt advocates for Leila in rooms she is not in,
and he is leaving. The no-stake role is filled only if Leila talks to Owen about more
than data.

**Cheapest tie to make her own:** Jae. The invitation already exists and costs one
email. Accept Owen if the learner argues that a no-stake person matters more before a
PI moves.

Learners usually miss the channel row. Because Leila asks only by direct message,
nobody in the consortium sees her work. The [Lab
norms]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/lab-norms/' | relative_url }}) page makes the same point
about questions kept out of the public channel.

## 2. Model first messages

**To Jae:**

> "Hi Jae, we talked at my poster in the spring about a distance-matched null for
> reciprocal pairs, and you suggested I email. Sorry this is late. I've since rerun it
> with distance preserved and most of the enrichment goes away; the notebook is here.
> One question: did you bin distance per cell type or across all cells? Happy to share
> the code if it helps with your volume."

**First channel post:**

> "Sharing in case it saves someone time: in the new version, thin-bridge merges in
> the region we work on are easier to catch in the side view than the top view. Does
> anyone have a list of the regions re-segmented between the last two versions?"

**To Dr. Mensah**, after telling Dr. Brandt: "I'd like to start sending Dr. Mensah
the analysis updates directly, copying you. Is that all right?"

> "Dr. Mensah, I'm the student running the connectivity analysis for our joint paper.
> With Dr. Brandt's agreement, I'll send updated figures to you directly from now on,
> so questions reach me faster. I'll send the next one Friday. One question now: does
> your group count a contact as a synapse only after two annotators agree?"

**To Ben:** "I'm glad to keep doing Thursdays. Could you read my methods section this
month? You'll see what's unclear to someone outside the lab."

Each message names a specific shared thing, asks one question answerable in a sentence,
and gives something. As the [Career mechanics]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/career-mechanics/' | relative_url }})
page puts it, a specific need stated aloud is what lets someone recognize they can help.

## 3. Private map: what good looks like

The map is not collected. Learners who share it tend to fall into two patterns.

**Strong:** several ties honestly marked **B**. At least one tie where flow is one-way,
noticed without self-blame. Empty roles circled. The three drafts go to real
initials, each with a specific opener.

**Weak:** a list of famous names the learner has met once, all marked **O**. Or
messages that open "I'd love to pick your brain", which asks the reader to design the
conversation. Ask: "What is the one question you want answered?"

## Feedback rubric

Score four dimensions **0–2 each**:

1. **Ties sorted by origin and flow.** 2 = every row has origin and both directions of
   flow; 1 = most rows; 0 = names and roles only.
2. **Messages are specific.** 2 = all three could only go to that reader; 1 = two of
   three; 0 = generic.
3. **One answerable question.** 2 = every message asks one question with a
   one-sentence answer; 1 = some; 0 = open asks such as "any advice?"
4. **Something offered back.** 2 = each message gives something; 1 = one or two do;
   0 = all asks.

A proficient response scores at least 6/8 with no zero on dimension 2. This is a local
teaching rubric, not a validated assessment instrument.

Do not grade the size of anyone's network, how senior their contacts are, or whether
they send the messages. Network size reflects access as much as effort, and grading it
would reward the learners who needed the session least.
