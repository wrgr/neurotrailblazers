*Workshop 4 of the [Professional Pathways workshops]({{ '/teaching/pathways/' | relative_url }}).
90 minutes, no slides required.*

- [Learner worksheet]({{ '/teaching/pathways/building-your-entourage-activity/' | relative_url }})
- [Instructor model responses]({{ '/teaching/pathways/building-your-entourage-answers/' | relative_url }})
- Background: [Career mechanics]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/career-mechanics/' | relative_url }}), especially
  *How collaborations form, and what one costs you*; [Lab norms]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/lab-norms/' | relative_url }}),
  especially *Ask in the public channel*

**Question:** How do I build a network of my own rather than borrowing my mentor's?

## Outcomes and preparation

Learners sort professional ties by how they started (through a mentor, or by their own
action) and by what flows each way. They test which ties would survive their mentor
leaving. They write three short first messages to people who already half-know them.
Each message names something specific, asks one question that can be answered in a
sentence, and offers something back. In private, they map their own ties and draft
three messages to real people.

[Module 24]({{ '/modules/module24/' | relative_url }}) builds a mentorship map with
five roles and teaches the cold email to a prospective supervisor. [Module
02]({{ '/modules/module02/' | relative_url }}) builds a support map, and [Module
23]({{ '/modules/module23/' | relative_url }}) covers the conference follow-up. This
workshop adds what those leave out: where each tie came from, whether it runs in both
directions, and the warm message that turns a borrowed or dormant tie into one the
learner owns. It uses the Module 24 roles rather than inventing new ones.

Before class, read the two background sections and the [MERIT
framework]({{ '/models/' | relative_url }}) stage on advanced research and
networking. Print the worksheet. **The case is invented.** Nobody names a real contact
aloud, and nobody sends anything during the session.

## Timed plan and facilitator cues

- **0–10 min:** follow-through from Charting Your Course: ask who ran the first week of
  a probe and what it showed. Take two answers. Then describe one professional tie that
  matters to you: whether it started through your mentor or through something you did,
  and what you have given back. Ask: "If your mentor left tomorrow, who would still
  answer your email?"
- **10–20 min:** borrowed versus own. Read the MERIT line: introductions made on a
  mentee's behalf are a mentor asset; conversations the mentee starts are the
  mentee's. Then read the career mechanics point that collaborations form when one
  person states a specific need and another recognizes they can meet it.
- **20–40 min:** pairs complete worksheet section 1. At minute 30, check that pairs
  are filling the *flows each way* column. Most first drafts list names and roles only,
  and miss that Leila gives Ben hours every week and asks for nothing.
- **40–50 min:** debrief. Ask which roles are empty. Expected: sponsor, since only Dr.
  Brandt fills it. Ask which tie is cheapest to make Leila's own. Expected: Jae or Owen.
- **50–65 min:** pairs complete section 2, three first messages.
- **65–80 min:** individuals complete section 3. Private writing. Circulate, but do
  not read over shoulders uninvited.
- **80–87 min:** pairs swap *one* of their section 2 messages. The partner applies the
  Module 24 test: could this have been sent to anyone else?
- **87–90 min:** exit ticket. Name the follow-through task and the date you will ask.

## Facilitator cautions

Networking costs fall unevenly. Conferences cost money that some learners must front
and others need not; the career mechanics page covers the reimbursement problem.
Caring responsibilities, disability and distance limit travel. Say plainly that a
public channel, a reading group or an email counts as much as a conference corridor.

Some learners have people they need to avoid. Do not ask why a name is missing from
anyone's map. If a learner says their only professional relationship is with a
supervisor who is the problem, do not solve it in the room. Point to the escalation
section of the lab norms page and to the
[Conflict]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/conflict/' | relative_url }}) page, and name a person
in your program they can talk to afterward.

Some learners worry that writing to a mentor's collaborator goes around the mentor. It
does not, as long as the mentor knows about the first message. Say so. Learners who
find networking transactional can hear the plain version: a tie where only one side
gives is a favor account, and it runs out.

## Assessment and follow-through

Use the four-dimension rubric on the model responses page. Collect sections 1 and 2;
section 3 stays with the learner unless they choose to share it.

**Follow-through:** send at least one of your three section 3 messages this week.
Record the reply, or the silence. Module 24's rule on silence applies: one short
follow-up after ten working days, then move on without concluding anything. Keep the
map; Future Forward asks for it. Start [Communicating Science
I]({{ '/teaching/pathways/communicating-science-1/' | relative_url }}) by asking who
sent a message and what came back.
