These responses use the worksheet's **invented** case. They are examples, not the only
acceptable wording. This page is public and suitable for formative feedback.

## 1. Cannot know yet, or have not asked?

| Option | Known now | Knowable in two weeks | Not knowable yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Extend interneuron analysis | The pipeline exists; Kai thinks it is quick. | Whether the new volume has the cell types proofread; whether anyone else is already doing it (ask Dr. Whitfield). | Whether the result will be interesting. |
| B. Synapse classifier | Rin enjoys the work; Dr. Kessler is keen; no labels exist. | How many hours of labeling a first model needs; whether another lab has labels to share (ask Dr. Kessler). | Whether it will beat the current detector. |
| C. Repeated contacts | The idea is published elsewhere; nobody here has run it. | Whether enough axons are proofread to count contacts; whether a simple version runs at all; whether Dr. Whitfield would support it. | Whether the effect exists in this volume. |

**C has the most removable uncertainty.** Three of its four unknowns take a small
pilot and one email. That makes C the best option to *probe* first. It does not make
C the best option to *choose*. The probe is cheap and tells Rin the most per day spent.

Rin's two weeks of reading did not move any cell from "knowable" to "known". The
[Meta-learning]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/meta-learning/' | relative_url }}) rule applies.
Rin could already state the question the next artifact answers, so more reading was
buying confidence. It was not buying information.

Accept A or B as the probe target if the learner argues from the table. For example,
B's labeling cost may be the single fact that decides it, and one conversation with
Dr. Kessler gets it.

## 2. Model probe

1. **Question:** are there enough well-proofread axons in the new volume to count
   repeated contacts at all?
2. **Do:** list axons marked proofread in one subregion; for up to 20, count targets
   that receive two or more synapses; shuffle each axon's synapses across nearby
   targets once, by script, and count again.
3. **Artifact:** the axon list, the notebook, and half a page on what proofreading the
   full analysis would need. Useful to the lab even if C is dropped.
4. **Stopping rule:** "If by day 10 I cannot find 20 axons proofread well enough to
   count contacts, I will park C and bring the half page to Dr. Whitfield. Otherwise I
   will bring both counts to lab meeting on day 14."
5. **Reversibility:** two weeks; stopping early costs little because the artifact
   stands on its own; it builds analysis skills that A also needs.

**Proposal to Dr. Whitfield:**

> "Before I choose, I'd like to spend two weeks checking whether C is feasible in the
> new volume. I'll count repeated contacts on about 20 proofread axons and bring the
> numbers and a note on proofreading needs to lab meeting at the end of the two weeks.
> Is that a good use of the time, or would you rather I choose now?"

The stopping rule is checkable by someone else, as in norm 22 on the [Technical
practice]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/technical-practice/' | relative_url }}) page. The
probe is a small pilot run while the plan can still change, which is norm 23 at the
scale of one person.

## 3. Direction memo: what good looks like

The memo is not collected. Learners who share it tend to fall into two patterns.

**Strong:** a narrow choice with a real date. At least one unknown moved into
"knowable in two weeks" with a named person or task. The weight line names someone
honestly ("mostly my supervisor's"). The wrong-choice sign is observable ("I have not
produced one figure I would show at lab meeting by March").

**Weak:** a choice as large as "what to do with my life", with no date. Every unknown
sits in "not knowable yet", which makes waiting look rational. The wrong-choice sign
is a feeling ("if I am unhappy"). Ask: "What is the smallest version of this choice
you face this month?"

## Feedback rubric

Score four dimensions **0–2 each**:

1. **Unknowns sorted.** 2 = each option has knowable-soon items tied to a task or
   person; 1 = some options; 0 = everything is "cannot know".
2. **Probe is bounded.** 2 = one question, fits two weeks, leaves an artifact; 1 = two
   of the three; 0 = the probe is "keep reading" or "try it and see".
3. **Stopping rule set in advance.** 2 = a date and an observable condition, with both
   branches stated; 1 = date or condition missing; 0 = no rule.
4. **Proposal can be answered.** 2 = the message to Dr. Whitfield asks a yes-or-no
   question with a date; 1 = clear plan, open question; 0 = no proposal.

A proficient response scores at least 6/8 with no zero on dimension 3. This is a local
teaching rubric, not a validated assessment instrument.

Do not grade which option the learner picks or which one their own memo leans toward.
The session teaches how to find out, not what to choose.
