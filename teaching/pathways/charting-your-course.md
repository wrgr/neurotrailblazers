*Workshop 3 of the [Professional Pathways workshops]({{ '/teaching/pathways/' | relative_url }}).
90 minutes, no slides required.*

- [Learner worksheet]({{ '/teaching/pathways/charting-your-course-activity/' | relative_url }})
- [Instructor model responses]({{ '/teaching/pathways/charting-your-course-answers/' | relative_url }})
- Background: [Meta-learning]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/meta-learning/' | relative_url }}), especially
  *When to stop reading and start doing*; [Technical practice]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/technical-practice/' | relative_url }}),
  especially *Family 5 — Commit before you look*

**Question:** How do I choose a direction when I cannot yet evaluate the options?

## Outcomes and preparation

Learners separate what they cannot know yet from what they have not looked up. They
design a probe of two weeks or less that answers one open question, leaves an artifact
behind, and has a stopping rule written in advance. They write a one-page direction
memo for a real choice of their own, with a decision date and a sign that would show
the choice was wrong.

[Module 24]({{ '/modules/module24/' | relative_url }}) covers career next steps: fit
matrices, outreach and application timing. It introduces reversibility as a decision
criterion. This workshop applies that criterion one level down, to the choice of a
research question inside a lab, and adds the probe. [Module
01]({{ '/modules/module01/' | relative_url }}) teaches learners to narrow a question;
this session is about choosing between questions before the evidence is in. Career
options come back in Future Forward, which asks learners to bring this memo.

Before class, read the two background sections and the worksheet case. Print the
worksheet. **The case is invented.** Learners choose whether their private memo is
about research, coursework or something else.

## Timed plan and facilitator cues

- **0–10 min:** follow-through from The Resilient STEM Scholar: ask who sent a status
  report on real work and what came back. Take two answers. Then tell the room about a
  direction you chose before you could evaluate it: what you could not have known, what
  you could have found out cheaply, and whether you did. Ask: "What would two weeks of
  trying have told you?"
- **10–20 min:** the move. Write on the board: *you cannot think your way to an
  evaluation you do not have the data for; you can buy the data cheaply.* Read the
  meta-learning rule: read until you can state the question the next artifact answers,
  then build it. Name the reversibility questions from Module 24: how long does this
  commit me, what does leaving early cost, what does it make easier next?
- **20–40 min:** pairs complete worksheet section 1. At minute 30, check that pairs
  have split "cannot know yet" from "has not asked". Most first drafts put "whether
  Dr. Whitfield would support option C" under cannot-know. It takes one email.
- **40–50 min:** debrief. For each unknown the room lists, ask: "Could Rin find this
  out in two weeks?" Expected: most of option C's unknowns yes; the long-term payoff
  of any option, no.
- **50–65 min:** pairs complete section 2, the probe.
- **65–80 min:** individuals complete section 3, the direction memo. Private writing.
  Circulate, but do not read over shoulders uninvited.
- **80–87 min:** pairs swap *Rin's probe*, not their own memo. The partner checks one
  thing: could someone other than Rin tell, on the stop date, whether the rule was met?
- **87–90 min:** exit ticket. Name the follow-through task and the date you will ask.

## Facilitator cautions

Do not favor the exciting option or the safe one. Learners hear both pieces of advice
often, and both skip the step this session teaches. Excitement says nothing about fit,
and safety says nothing about value. A probe tests either.

Some learners have no free choice. Their project is fixed by a grant, a visa or a
funding line. Do not ask anyone to say which applies. Tell the room the memo still
works on smaller choices inside a fixed project: which analysis first, which method,
which collaborator. Reversibility depends on circumstances you cannot see, so the
memo stays private.

Supervisors reproduce their own path by default. The [MERIT
framework]({{ '/models/' | relative_url }}) calls this the single-path assumption.
Say that the memo is input to a conversation with the supervisor, not a way around
one. If a learner says their supervisor will not discuss direction at all, point to
the [Conflict]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/conflict/' | relative_url }}) page and to a
named person they can talk to afterward.

## Assessment and follow-through

Use the four-dimension rubric on the model responses page. Collect sections 1 and 2;
section 3 stays with the learner unless they choose to share it.

**Follow-through:** run the first week of your probe, or, if your memo is not about
research, take the first concrete step it names. Show the memo to your supervisor or
one other mentor and write down one thing they would change. Keep the memo; Future
Forward asks for it. Start [Building Your STEM
Entourage]({{ '/teaching/pathways/building-your-entourage/' | relative_url }}) by
asking who ran the probe and what it showed.
