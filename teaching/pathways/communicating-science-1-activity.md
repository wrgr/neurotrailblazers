**Time:** 20 minutes in pairs, 18 minutes alone, an 8-minute cold-reader swap, then 14
minutes of private writing. The case below is **invented**. Rosa and Dr. Kimura are not real people,
the volume and "release T12" are fictional, and the numbers are made up for practice.

## The case

Rosa is a second-year student in Dr. Kimura's connectomics lab. Here is her result, as
she wrote it in her lab notebook:

> Fictional mouse cortex EM volume, layer 2/3, release T12. 58 inhibitory neurons with
> fully proofread axons, 1,904 synapses onto
> excitatory neurons. Synapses land on the cell body or the first 20 µm of dendrite 2.7×
> more often than a null model that keeps each neuron's synapse count but places the
> synapses at random over the same partners' membrane. On 214 partly proofread neurons
> the ratio is 2.1×, with a wider interval. "Inhibitory" is from synapse shape, not
> from a molecular marker.

Rosa then wrote three short drafts for three audiences.

- **Draft A, for lab meeting:** "Proofread inhibitory axons show strong perisomatic
  targeting (2.7× vs. null), confirming the perisomatic control hypothesis."
- **Draft B, for a physiologist on her committee:** "We found that inhibitory neurons
  prefer to contact the cell body of excitatory neurons, the site where they can most
  effectively veto firing."
- **Draft C, for the department newsletter:** "Scientists have found the brain's off
  switch: special cells wrap around neurons and stop them firing, which may help explain
  epilepsy."

## 1. Find the invariants (8 minutes, pairs)

From the notebook entry only, write down:

- what was measured, in one sentence;
- the data and version it came from;
- what it was compared against;
- which [claim bin]({{ '/technical-training/01-why-map-the-brain/' | relative_url }})
  the measurement sits in (A, B or C), and which part needs an assumption;
- **one non-claim**: a sentence Rosa must refuse to write in any register.

## 2. Find the word that moved (12 minutes, pairs)

For each draft, underline the single word or phrase that changes the claim most. Then
say what the notebook actually supports in its place. Also note one invariant each draft
drops. Which draft did the most damage, and why that one?

## 3. Three registers (18 minutes, alone)

Rewrite Rosa's result three times. Each version must contain the measurement, the
comparison, and the non-claim in words that audience will understand.

1. **Peer** (a connectomics researcher): two or three sentences. Include the version and
   the proofreading state.
2. **Adjacent scientist** (a physiologist or a computational neuroscientist): three or
   four sentences. Name any assumption in the same sentence as the claim it supports.
3. **Public** (a curious adult with no biology): three or four sentences. One number at
   most. No "off switch".

**Invariance check.** Put the three non-claims next to each other. Are they logically
the same sentence in different words, or has one become weaker?

## Cold-reader swap (8 minutes)

Give your partner **only** your public version. Your partner writes two lines without
talking to you: *what was found* and *what was not shown*. Compare with your section 1
invariants. Where did they drift? Then your partner answers one more question: *could
a reader repeat the non-claim back to you?* Revise one sentence before you move on.

## 4. Your own result (14 minutes, private)

This section is yours. You do not have to share it. If you have no result yet, use a
paper you have read.

1. Write the invariants for one result: measurement, data and version, comparison, bin,
   non-claim.
2. Write it in all three registers.
3. Circle the word in your public version you are most tempted to strengthen. Write what
   it would claim if you did.

## Exit ticket

Finish both sentences: "The word I am most tempted to strengthen is ___. The sentence I will not
write is ___."

**Follow-through this week:** give your public version to one person outside your field.
Ask them to say back what you found and what you did not. Note where they drifted. Bring
all three registers to [Communicating Science
II]({{ '/teaching/pathways/communicating-science-2/' | relative_url }}).

**Success criteria:** the invariants are listed before any rewrite; each draft's
distorting word is named with its supported replacement; all three registers carry the
same non-claim; assumptions are named where they are used.
