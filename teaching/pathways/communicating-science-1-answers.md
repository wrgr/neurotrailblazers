These responses use the worksheet's **invented** case. They are examples, not the only
acceptable wording. This page is public and suitable for formative feedback.

## 1. The invariants

- **Measured:** where inhibitory synapses land on excitatory neurons, counted.
- **Data:** one mouse, one volume, layer 2/3, materialization version 1078; 58 fully
  proofread axons, 1,904 synapses.
- **Compared against:** a null that keeps each neuron's synapse count and places synapses
  at random over the same partners. Result 2.7×; 2.1× on partly proofread axons.
- **Bin:** the location count is Bin A. "Inhibitory" is Bin B: it assumes synapse shape
  predicts transmitter. Anything about firing, control or behavior is Bin C.
- **Non-claim:** "This does not show that these synapses stop, control or time the
  firing of the neurons they contact." A second acceptable one: "This does not show the
  pattern holds in other layers, animals or species."

## 2. The word that moved

| Draft | Word | What the notebook supports | Invariant dropped |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "confirming the … control hypothesis" | "consistent with" a targeting pattern; control is not measured | what the null preserves, and the 2.1× on partly proofread axons |
| B | "veto firing" (and "prefer") | "land on or near the cell body more than chance"; efficacy needs a biophysical model | version and proofreading state; the Bin B assumption behind "inhibitory" |
| C | "stop them firing" | a count of where synapses sit | everything: data, comparison, non-claim |

**The most damaging draft** is C, but not because of "off switch". The metaphor is loose;
"stop them firing" is a Bin C claim stated as fact, and "may help explain epilepsy" adds
a clinical link nothing in the notebook touches. Accept draft A as the answer if the
learner argues that a specialist audience will repeat "confirming" in their own talks,
which spreads the error further. That is the level 2 defect on the
[Reading and judging]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/reading-and-judging/' | relative_url }})
page: the framing goes further than the measurement.

"Prefer" in draft B is worth a sentence. It is common usage and most peers read it as
"more than chance". A public reader hears choice. Keep it for peers only, if at all.

## 3. Model registers

**Peer:** "In layer 2/3 of one mouse visual cortex volume (materialization v1078), the
58 inhibitory neurons with fully proofread axons placed synapses on or near excitatory
cell bodies 2.7× more often than a synapse-count-preserving null; 2.1× on partly
proofread axons. Inhibitory identity is morphological. We make no claim about effect
on firing."

**Adjacent scientist:** "We counted where the synapses of 58 fully reconstructed
inhibitory neurons land on their excitatory partners. They land on or near the cell body
about 2.7 times more often than if they were scattered at random over the same cells.
We call them inhibitory from synapse shape, which is usually but not always right. The
data are anatomy only, so they say nothing yet about whether these synapses change when
the partner fires. That is a question for recordings."

**Public:** "We traced the connections of a few dozen brain cells in a tiny piece of
mouse brain, using electron microscope images. One kind of cell, thought to quiet its
neighbors, attaches close to the center of those neighbors almost three times more often
than chance would give. A wiring map shows where the connections are, not what they do,
so we do not yet know whether these connections change what the neighbors do."

The three non-claims are the same claim: position, not function. The public version
spends its one number on "almost three times" and drops the version, which is
acceptable because the version is in the peer text and is not needed to state the claim.

## 4. Personal result: what good looks like

The private section is not collected. Learners who share it tend to show two patterns.

**Strong:** the invariants are written first, the non-claim names a specific stronger
claim ("does not show these neurons are the same type in the other hemisphere"), and the
circled word comes with an honest note about why it is tempting ("because 'reveals'
sounds like a finding").

**Weak:** the non-claim is generic ("more research is needed") or restates the limitation
of all anatomy without saying what this result would be mistaken for. The public version
is the peer version with jargon removed, so the reader cannot tell what was compared to
what.

## Feedback rubric

Score four dimensions **0–2 each**:

1. **Invariants stated.** 2 = measurement, data with version, comparison, bin and
   non-claim all present; 1 = three or four of them; 0 = fewer, or a rewrite began first.
2. **Distortion diagnosed.** 2 = each draft's moving word is named with a supported
   replacement; 1 = words named without replacements; 0 = only "off switch" found.
3. **Registers differ in vocabulary, not claim.** 2 = all three read as written for their
   audience and carry the same measurement; 1 = one register is a trimmed copy of
   another; 0 = the public version claims function.
4. **Non-claim intact.** 2 = the same non-claim, logically equivalent, in all three;
   1 = present in all three but weaker in one; 0 = missing from any register.

A proficient response scores at least 6/8 with no zero on dimension 4. This is a local
teaching rubric, not a validated assessment instrument.

Do not grade prose style, humor or how vivid the public version is. A plain public
version with the non-claim intact beats a lively one without it.
