90 minutes, no slides required.*

**Question:** How do I make my work legible to people outside it, without flattening it?

## Outcomes and preparation

Learners list the parts of a result that must not change between audiences; find the
single word that changes a claim in a draft; write one result for a peer, an adjacent
scientist and a member of the public; and check that the non-claim survives all three.

[Module 17]({{ '/modules/module17/' | relative_url }}) teaches calibrated language in a
paper, for one audience of specialists. [Module 22]({{ '/modules/module22/' | relative_url }})
asks for a caveat written twice, for a specialist and a non-specialist, and its
[public-engagement companion]({{ '/teaching/module22-public-engagement/' | relative_url }})
adds the sentence "This evidence does not yet show…". This workshop puts three versions
side by side and audits the drift between them. It adds the middle register, the adjacent
scientist, which is where most distortion enters, and a cold-reader test of the public
version. It does not teach slides or talks. That is the next workshop.

Before class, read the Unit 01 bins and the worksheet case. Print the worksheet. Bring
one sentence about your own work that you have seen simplified badly, in a press release,
a grant summary or your own draft. **The case is invented.** Learners may use their own
result in section 4, but nobody is asked to read unpublished work aloud.

## Timed plan and facilitator cues

- **0–8 min:** follow-through from workshop 4: ask who sent a first message and what
  came back. Then read your own sentence in two versions, one that kept the claim and one
  that changed it. Ask: "Which word moved the claim?" Collect guesses on the board.
- **8–18 min:** the invariant set. Write on the board what Module 22 says must not
  change: what was measured, on what data, at what version, and what it does not
  establish. Add the bins: a Bin A count cannot carry a Bin C sentence in any register.
  Say plainly that vocabulary, analogy and depth may change. The claim may not.
- **18–38 min:** pairs complete sections 1 and 2 on the invented case. At minute 28,
  check that pairs have found the verb in draft C that turned a count into a function
  claim ("stop them firing"). Most pairs find "off switch" first and stop there.
- **38–46 min:** debrief section 2. Ask each pair for one word from draft B. Expect
  "prefer" and "veto". Ask: "Is 'veto' wrong, or is it an unnamed assumption?" It is the
  second, which makes it a Bin B claim missing its assumption.
- **46–64 min:** individuals complete section 3, the three rewrites. Circulate and read
  the public version first. If it has no non-claim, the other two usually have a weak one.
- **64–72 min:** cold-reader swap. Partners read *only* the public version and write
  what was found and what was not. Compare against the section 1 invariants.
- **72–86 min:** section 4, private writing on the learner's own result or on a paper
  they have read. Do not read over shoulders uninvited.
- **86–90 min:** exit ticket. Remind learners to bring their three registers to the next
  workshop.

## Facilitator cautions

Do not teach that public communication means removing caveats, or that caveats make a
public version worse. Module 22 names this as a misconception. The public non-claim can
be one plain sentence.

Do not let the session turn into a critique of journalists. The case drafts are all
written by the scientist, which is where most distortion starts.

Learners who write in a second language may produce plainer public versions than native
speakers and underrate them. Grade the claim, not the fluency. Learners without a result
of their own should use a paper from the [Journal
Club]({{ '/technical-training/journal-club/' | relative_url }}) in section 4.

## Assessment and follow-through

Use the four-dimension rubric on the model responses page. Collect sections 1 to 3.
Section 4 stays with the learner unless they choose to share it.

**Follow-through:** give the public version of your own result to one person outside
your field this week and ask them to say back what you found and what you did not. Note
where they drifted. Start [Communicating Science
II]({{ '/teaching/pathways/communicating-science-2/' | relative_url }}) by asking who did
it and what the listener got wrong. Learners bring all three registers to that session.
