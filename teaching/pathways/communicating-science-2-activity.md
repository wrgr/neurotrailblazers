**Time:** 12 minutes in pairs, 30 minutes alone, 20 minutes in threes, then 8 minutes of
private writing. The case below is **invented**. Rosa, Dr. Kimura and the questioners are
not real people. Bring your three registers from [Communicating Science
I]({{ '/teaching/pathways/communicating-science-1/' | relative_url }}), or use Rosa's result.

## The case

Rosa (from the previous workshop) gives a five-minute talk at a department data blitz on
her inhibitory-synapse result: synapses land on or near excitatory cell bodies 2.7× more
often than a null predicts, at materialization version 1078, 2.1× on partly proofread
axons. Here is the Q&A, as a friend wrote it down.

1. **Q:** "Sorry, what's a materialization version?"
   **Rosa** (about two minutes): explains the proofreading database, how edits
   accumulate, why tables are frozen, and a problem the lab had last spring.
2. **Q:** "Proofreaders usually fix axons near cell bodies first. Couldn't that
   make the targeting?"
   **Rosa:** "Our proofreading is very thorough, so I don't think so."
3. **Q:** "So does this explain epilepsy?"
   **Rosa:** "It could well be relevant, yes. These cells are known to be involved."
4. **Q:** "We showed something like this in hippocampus a few years ago."
   **Rosa:** argues for a minute that hippocampus is different. The chair signals time.
5. **Q:** "Isn't this something anatomists described a long time ago?"
   **Rosa:** "Um. I guess. Yes."

## 1. Diagnose the Q&A (12 minutes, pairs)

For each question, write:

- its type: clarification, methods challenge, alternative explanation, scope,
  positioning, "so what", or a comment that is not a question;
- what went wrong: too long, defensive, over-conceding, answered a different question,
  or did not stop;
- one sentence Rosa should have said instead.

Then answer: which answer cost Rosa the most with this room, and why that one?

## 2. Your five-minute outline (18 minutes, alone)

Use your own result or Rosa's. Fill every slot. No slides.

| Time | Slot | What you will say, in note form |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | Question and why it matters | |
| 0:45–1:45 | Data and method, with a one-line provenance statement | |
| 1:45–3:45 | The result, with its uncertainty rung | |
| 3:45–4:30 | Limitation and the non-claim | |
| 4:30–5:00 | Next step | |

Write your opener in full: question, stake, news, and the challenge you expect, named
first. Read it against a timer. If it runs past 45 seconds, cut background first.

## 3. Your hard-question bank (12 minutes, alone)

Write **eight** questions someone could ask about your talk. Include at least one of
each: methods challenge, alternative explanation, scope, positioning, "so what", and one
you cannot yet answer. Label each with its type.

For your three hardest, write an answer you could say in 40 seconds or less. Each answer
ends with a next test, a boundary, or a stop, not a defense.

## 4. Live round (20 minutes, threes)

First, two minutes: pass your question bank to the person on your left, who adds one
question you did not think of. Then take turns as speaker, questioner and timekeeper,
six minutes per round.

- The speaker gives the 90-second opener.
- The questioner asks two questions: one chosen from the speaker's bank, one of their own.
- The speaker names each question's type aloud before answering.
- The timekeeper notes the length of each answer and whether the speaker stopped.
- One minute of feedback: which answer would a room trust more, and why?

Questions are hard on the work, never on the person.

## 5. Your own pattern (8 minutes, private)

This section is yours. You do not have to share it.

1. Under pressure, do you tend to defend, concede, or keep talking? Give one example.
2. Which question in your bank do you most hope nobody asks? What would it reveal?
3. What would you need to know, or do, to answer it in 40 seconds?

## Exit ticket

Exit ticket: "The question I will rehearse aloud is ___. I will ask ___ to put it to me by
___ (date)."

**Follow-through this week:** ask someone more senior to put your hardest question to you
live. Add the question they asked instead to the bank. [The Savvy
Researcher]({{ '/teaching/pathways/savvy-researcher/' | relative_url }}) starts by asking
what question you were not expecting.

**Success criteria:** every transcript question is typed before it is judged; the outline
gives the limitation and non-claim their own time; the bank covers every type and includes
one question you cannot yet answer; live answers name the type and stop.
