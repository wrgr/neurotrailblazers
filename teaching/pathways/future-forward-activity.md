**Time:** 30 minutes in pairs, then 25 minutes alone. The case below is **invented**.
Hana, Dr. Castillo and Luis are not real people.

**Bring:** your direction memo from [Charting Your
Course]({{ '/teaching/pathways/charting-your-course/' | relative_url }}) and your network
map from [Building Your STEM
Entourage]({{ '/teaching/pathways/building-your-entourage/' | relative_url }}).

## The case

It is September. Hana has worked for two years as a research technician in Dr. Castillo's
connectomics lab, proofreading and running the lab's synapse-table exports. Her contract
ends next August. Here is what she knows:

- For fourteen months she has been "thinking about" doctoral applications. The programs
  she likes close in early December. She has not contacted any prospective supervisors.
- In June, Dr. Castillo said in passing: "We'll probably be able to renew you." Hana has
  not asked what pays for her position. The grant that does is up for renewal, with a
  decision expected in April.
- Luis, a former labmate, now works on an imaging team at a company. He says the team
  hires continuously and he would refer her.
- Dr. Castillo and a postdoc have watched her work for two years. Neither has been asked
  for a letter.
- Hana writes in her notebook: "I'll decide once I know more."

## 1. Name the drift (8 minutes)

Write the path Hana is on if she decides nothing. Then list each date on which one of
her options closes or gets much harder. Circle the date on which "decide later" stops
being a real choice.

## 2. Evaluate the options (14 minutes)

Evaluate three options: **stay and seek renewal**, **apply to doctoral programs this
cycle**, **apply for the industry role**. For each, fill in these columns, in this order:

- **Reversibility:** how long it commits her, what leaving early costs, what it makes
  easier next.
- **Cost:** money, time, and what she gives up.
- **What it demands now:** evidence she has, and the gap.
- **Biggest unknown:** and the one conversation that would resolve it, with whom.

Only then write one line on fit. You may combine options if a combination is honest
about its cost.

## 3. Hana's decision memo (8 minutes)

Write five lines for Hana: what she decides; why, citing your table; what she gives up;
what would tell her it was wrong; and the date she will review it.

## 4. Your options and twelve-month plan (25 minutes, private)

This section is yours. You do not have to share it.

1. From your direction memo, write your default path and the date it becomes
   irreversible.
2. Evaluate two or three options, including the default, with the four columns above.
3. Write your decision in one sentence. If you cannot decide yet, write the date you
   will, and what you will find out first.
4. Draft twelve months, by quarter. Each action gets a date, a lead time if it has one,
   and, where you can, a name from your network map.
5. Add one learning commitment for outside any course: one skill, the artifact that
   will show it, and a stopping rule for reading about it, as in
   [Meta-learning]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/meta-learning/' | relative_url }}).

Salaries, hiring rates and visa rules are not on this worksheet on purpose. Where your
plan depends on one, write "find out" and the person or source you will ask.

## Peer check and exit ticket

Swap only your decision sentence and your first quarter with your partner. Your partner
answers one question: *does every action have a date?* Add a date to one that does not.

Exit ticket: "My first action is ___, by ___ (date). My checkpoint is ___ (date)."

**Follow-through this week:** take the first action. Put the checkpoint in your
calendar with a note to reread this page's section 4 on that day.

**Success criteria:** the default path has a date; options are judged on reversibility
and cost before fit; the decision memo says what would show it was wrong; each plan
action is dated and, where possible, names a person.
