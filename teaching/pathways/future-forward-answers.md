These responses use the worksheet's **invented** case. They are examples, not the only
acceptable decision. This page is public and suitable for formative feedback.

## 1. The drift

Hana's default is to stay and wait for Dr. Brandt's renewal. If she decides nothing, she
learns in April whether she has a job, with four months left and the doctoral cycle
already closed. The default is not "keep options open". It quietly closes one.

The dates: **mid-to-late October**, the last sensible point to contact supervisors and
ask referees four to six weeks ahead, as [Module 24]({{ '/modules/module24/' | relative_url }})
advises; **early December**, applications close; **April**, the renewal decision;
**August**, the contract ends. Circle October. After that, "decide later" means "not
this cycle".

## 2. The options

| | Stay, seek renewal | Doctoral programs this cycle | Industry role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reversibility** | High, but only if renewed; the risk sits with the grant | Low once enrolled; applying is fully reversible | Moderate; returning to doctoral study later is common, harder as pay rises |
| **Cost** | A year with no new credential; the April uncertainty | Fees, autumn evenings, several years at trainee pay if it goes ahead | Time away from the publication record; translating her experience |
| **Demands now** | Nothing, which is the problem | Supervisor contacts, a statement built on her work, two letters | A portfolio a non-neuroscientist can read |
| **Biggest unknown** | Whether the renewal will pay for her; ask Dr. Brandt | Whether any supervisor has a fitting project; email three | What the team selects on; ask Luis |

**Fit, last:** her two years of proofreading and exports suit both research and imaging
roles. That is why fit alone cannot decide this.

The pattern to point out: *applying* is the reversible step; *enrolling* is not. The
industry posting stays open. The doctoral window does not. That asymmetry, not a
preference, is what orders the decision. [Career
mechanics]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/career-mechanics/' | relative_url }}) adds the reason
her letters matter: two people have watched her work for two years, which takes two
years to build.

## 3. Model decision memo

> "Decision: I will apply to four doctoral programs this cycle, and this week I will ask
> Dr. Brandt what pays for my post. Why: the doctoral window closes in December; the
> industry team hires all year and staying depends on an April decision I cannot
> influence. I give up a quiet autumn and the fees. I was wrong if, by 15 November, none
> of the supervisors I contacted has a project I would want; then I stop and call Luis.
> Review: 15 November, and again when offers or the renewal decision arrive."

Accept an industry-first memo if it states its cost and reversibility, for example
"take the industry role, apply in two years, keep one referee warm". Do not accept "I
will decide once I know more" without naming what and by when.

## 4. Own plan: what good looks like

**Strong:** a named default with a date; one decision sentence; four quarters with
dated actions, lead times on anything with a deadline, and names from the network map;
one learning commitment with an artifact and a stopping rule; "find out" written where
a number or rule is unknown.

**Weak:** a list of goals with no dates ("get better at Python", "network more");
every action routed through one supervisor; a plan with no checkpoint, which is drift
written down.

## Feedback rubric

Score four dimensions **0–2 each**, on sections 1–3:

1. **Drift named.** 2 = default path and the date it closes an option; 1 = path without
   date; 0 = no default named.
2. **Reversibility and cost before fit.** 2 = all three options scored on both; 1 = some;
   0 = options ranked by preference.
3. **Decision on record.** 2 = decision, reason, cost, a sign of being wrong and a review
   date; 1 = three of these; 0 = "decide later".
4. **Unknowns turned into conversations.** 2 = each biggest unknown has a named person;
   1 = some; 0 = unknowns left as worries.

A proficient response scores at least 6/8 with no zero on dimension 3. This is a local
teaching rubric, not a validated assessment instrument.

Do not grade which option a learner chooses for Hana or for themselves, and do not
grade section 4. A well-made decision to leave research scores the same as one to stay.
