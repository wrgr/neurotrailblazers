*Workshop 10 of the [Professional Pathways workshops]({{ '/teaching/pathways/' | relative_url }}).
90 minutes, no slides required.*

- [Learner worksheet]({{ '/teaching/pathways/future-forward-activity/' | relative_url }})
- [Instructor model responses]({{ '/teaching/pathways/future-forward-answers/' | relative_url }})
- Background: [Career mechanics]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/career-mechanics/' | relative_url }}),
  especially *Hiring: academia and industry are different machines*;
  [Meta-learning]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/meta-learning/' | relative_url }}), especially
  *When to stop reading and start doing*

**Question:** What comes next, and how do I decide rather than drift?

## Outcomes and preparation

Learners name the path they will take if they decide nothing, and the date on which not
deciding becomes a decision. They evaluate two or three options on reversibility and cost
before fit; write a short decision memo with a checkpoint and a sign that would tell them
they were wrong; and draft a twelve-month plan in which each action has a date, a lead
time and, where possible, a named person.

[Module 24]({{ '/modules/module24/' | relative_url }}) and its [session
kit]({{ '/teaching/sessions/module24/' | relative_url }}) build the fit matrix, the
decision table and the outreach messages. This workshop assumes that material exists or
can be sketched. What it adds is the decision itself: naming drift as an option with a
deadline, committing on record, and setting the date to review. It also covers the
second half of MERIT stage 6, a plan for learning outside a structured program. The
portfolio those plans point at is [Module 25]({{ '/modules/module25/' | relative_url }}).

Learners bring two earlier artifacts: the direction memo from [Charting Your
Course]({{ '/teaching/pathways/charting-your-course/' | relative_url }}) and the network
map from [Building Your STEM
Entourage]({{ '/teaching/pathways/building-your-entourage/' | relative_url }}). Remind
them a week ahead. Before class, read the Module 24 decision table and the worksheet case.
**The case is invented.** Nobody is asked to state their options, finances or
immigration status aloud.

## Timed plan and facilitator cues

- **0–10 min:** follow-through from STEM Identity and Purpose: ask who made their first
  contribution and what happened next. Take two answers. Then tell the room about one
  path you did not take, and which parts of your own route were contingent rather than
  earned. MERIT stage 6 names the risk this guards against: mentors reproduce their own
  trajectory by default. Ask: "What did you assume was the next step before anyone
  asked you?"
- **10–18 min:** draw a timeline on the board with today, three deadlines and a contract
  end. Say: "Not deciding is an option. It has a deadline too." Then write Module 24's
  three reversibility questions: how long does it commit me, what does leaving early
  cost, and what does it make easier next.
- **18–48 min:** pairs complete worksheet sections 1, 2 and 3 on the invented case. At
  minute 30, check that pairs have filled the reversibility and cost columns *before*
  deciding which option is best. Most pairs start by picking a winner. Also check that
  somebody has written the date on which Hana's default becomes irreversible.
- **48–55 min:** debrief section 3. Ask one pair to read Hana's decision memo. Test it:
  "Two years from now, could Hana tell from this whether she chose well?"
- **55–80 min:** individuals complete section 4 with their direction memo and network map.
  This is private writing. Circulate, but do not read over shoulders uninvited.
- **80–87 min:** pairs swap *only the decision sentence and the first quarter of the
  plan*. The partner checks one thing: does every action have a date?
- **87–90 min:** exit ticket. Name the follow-through task.

## Facilitator cautions

Do not present a doctoral program as the default or as success. The single-path
assumption is the failure mode this stage exists to prevent. Leaving research is a
legitimate outcome of a good decision.

Visa rules, funding eligibility and family finances often decide which options are
open. Do not guess at them in the room, and do not ask. Point to an institutional
adviser, as Module 24 does. Do not quote salaries or hiring rates; Career mechanics
explains why and what to look up instead.

Some learners will arrive without the earlier artifacts. Give them five minutes at the
start of section 4 to write two sentences on the direction they lean toward and to list
five people they could ask. Do not hold up the room.

## Assessment and follow-through

Use the four-dimension rubric on the model responses page. Collect sections 1 to 3.
Section 4 stays with the learner unless they choose to share it.

**Follow-through:** take the first dated action in the plan within a week, and put the
decision checkpoint in a calendar. This is the last workshop, so there is no next
session to open with the question. Hold a short closing meeting two to four weeks later
and ask who did it and what happened. Then return to the [Professional Pathways
workshops]({{ '/teaching/pathways/' | relative_url }}) hub to decide which sessions the
cohort should repeat or run again with the next group.
