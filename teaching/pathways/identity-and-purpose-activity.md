**Time:** 25 minutes in pairs, then 20 minutes alone. The case below is **invented**.
Inés and Dev are not real people. Nobody will ask you whether you feel you belong.

**The test,** from [Belonging]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/belonging/' | relative_url }}):
when the feeling arrives, write down the specific thing you did not know. If you can
name it, it is probably a knowledge gap, and it closes. If you cannot, look harder or
ask. If you named it, closed it, and the feeling did not move, take that seriously.

## The case

Inés is a first-year graduate student in a connectomics lab. Nobody in her family has
worked in research. Here is her first month, in her own notes:

1. Week 1, lab meeting: "Everyone talked about 'the new materialization' and 'ID churn'.
   I said nothing. I went home thinking I'm not meant to be here."
2. Week 2: "I spent five hours preparing for a twenty-minute one-on-one. Other people
   seem to walk in cold. I must be behind."
3. Week 2: "Someone mentioned their stipend comes from Dr. Novak's grant. I thought
   the department paid us. Everyone else seemed to know."
4. Week 3, seminar: "I had a question and didn't ask it. Someone else asked the same
   thing and the speaker said it was a great question."
5. Weeks 3 and 4: "My proofreading on 30 axons was shown at lab meeting by another
   student as his work, twice. When I mentioned it to him he said I was being touchy.
   The lab manager said that's just how he is, and I should work around it."
6. Week 4, journal club: "I felt wrong the whole hour. I can't say why."

Dev joined the same lab after eight years as a software engineer. His notes say:
"Meetings are slow. I can't follow the biology, but that's a detail I'll pick up later.
Honestly I don't think I fit here."

## 1. Sort the moments (15 minutes)

For each of Inés's six moments, write:

- The specific thing she did not know, if it can be named.
- **Gap**, **environment signal**, or **cannot tell yet**.
- For *cannot tell yet*: the one question that would decide it, and whom she asks.

Then write one line for Dev: what is his actual gap, and what is the trap in "a detail
I'll pick up later"?

## 2. Inés's contribution plan (10 minutes)

Write three contributions Inés could make in the next four weeks. Each must be
**checkable**: someone else could confirm it happened. Give each a date and say what
evidence it leaves behind.

Then, separately, write what Inés should do about moment 5. It is not a fourth
contribution.

## 3. Your own diagnosis and plan (20 minutes, private)

This section is yours. You do not have to share the diagnosis, and it is not collected.

**Diagnosis.** Pick one recent moment when you felt out of place in your research
setting. Write the specific thing you did not know. Classify it: gap, environment
signal, or cannot tell yet. Write your next step: where you will get the missing thing
and by when, or which question you will ask and of whom. If you notice several
environment signals, write the name of one person outside the situation you could talk
to.

**Contribution plan.** Write three checkable contributions you will make in the next
four weeks, each with a date and the evidence it leaves. You may share this part.

## Peer check and exit ticket

Swap only your contribution plan with your partner, not your diagnosis. Your partner
answers one question: *could someone else verify each of these happened?* Revise one
contribution that fails.

Exit ticket: "My first contribution is ___. I will make it by ___ (date)."

**Follow-through this week:** make that first contribution. If your diagnosis named a
gap, close it, and note whether the feeling moved.

**Success criteria:** each moment is sorted with the named thing written down;
environment signals are neither explained away nor over-called; contributions are
checkable and dated; moment 5 gets a response that is not "contribute more".

If something today landed hard, talk to one of the people your facilitator named. This
worksheet is not counseling.
