Start here if you want to teach the professional side of research training as
sessions rather than as advice. These **ten 90-minute workshops** each have a timed
plan with facilitator cues, a learner worksheet built around an invented case, and
public model responses with a feedback rubric. They were developed under COMPASS
naming; the [education models]({{ '/models/' | relative_url }}#professional-pathways-workshops)
page now calls them the Professional Pathways workshops.

Nothing here needs slides, accounts or data. Each plan runs from the page, a
whiteboard and the printed worksheet. The depth behind every session lives in the
[hidden curriculum]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/' | relative_url }}); the workshops turn
those pages into something a cohort practices and produces.

## The ten sessions

Each workshop produces one written artifact. Learners keep them in a single folder;
several later sessions ask them to bring an earlier one back.

| # | Workshop | Question | Artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Orientation]({{ '/teaching/pathways/orientation/' | relative_url }}) | What is expected of me here, and how do I find out what is not written down? | Expectations list and week-four check-in |
| 2 | [The Resilient STEM Scholar]({{ '/teaching/pathways/resilient-scholar/' | relative_url }}) | What do I do when the work fails, and how do I tell a failing project from a failing me? | Failure diagnosis and a bad-week report |
| 3 | [Charting Your Course in Research]({{ '/teaching/pathways/charting-your-course/' | relative_url }}) | How do I choose a direction when I cannot yet evaluate the options? | Direction memo with a reversible probe |
| 4 | [Building Your STEM Entourage]({{ '/teaching/pathways/building-your-entourage/' | relative_url }}) | How do I build a network of my own rather than borrowing my mentor's? | Network map and three first messages |
| 5 | [Communicating Science I]({{ '/teaching/pathways/communicating-science-1/' | relative_url }}) | How do I make my work legible to people outside it, without flattening it? | One result in three registers, non-claim intact |
| 6 | [Communicating Science II]({{ '/teaching/pathways/communicating-science-2/' | relative_url }}) | How do I defend the work live, under questions I did not choose? | Five-minute talk outline and a hard-question bank |
| 7 | [The Savvy Researcher]({{ '/teaching/pathways/savvy-researcher/' | relative_url }}) | How does the system actually work — funding, authorship, review, credit? | Authorship memo and a system map |
| 8 | [Professional Conduct in STEM]({{ '/teaching/pathways/professional-conduct/' | relative_url }}) | What are my obligations, and what do I do when I see them breached? | Scenario rulings and an error report |
| 9 | [STEM Identity and Purpose]({{ '/teaching/pathways/identity-and-purpose/' | relative_url }}) | Do I belong here, and what would it mean if the answer is complicated? | A contribution plan and a private diagnosis |
| 10 | [Future Forward]({{ '/teaching/pathways/future-forward/' | relative_url }}) | What comes next, and how do I decide rather than drift? | Evaluated options and a twelve-month plan |

## When to run them

The numbering is the order the materials are written to run in. Each session also maps
to a stage of the [MERIT framework]({{ '/models/' | relative_url }}), shown below, and
the series passes through those stages roughly twice. A cohort does not need all ten, and it
should not receive them as a block at the end of technical training. The norms are
needed first, not last.

| MERIT stage | Run these |
|---|---|
| Selection and orientation | 1 Orientation, 9 STEM Identity and Purpose |
| Skill development | 2 The Resilient STEM Scholar, 5 Communicating Science I |
| Independent research | 3 Charting Your Course, 6 Communicating Science II |
| Advanced research | 4 Building Your STEM Entourage, 7 The Savvy Researcher |
| Career transition | 10 Future Forward, 8 Professional Conduct |

Two exceptions to that order are deliberate. Run **Professional Conduct** early if
your cohort is about to join active labs; it appears late in the MERIT pairing only
because the career-transition stage is when learners start supervising others.
Run **STEM Identity and Purpose** in the first month, not after someone is already
deciding whether to leave.

**Spacing.** One workshop every two to four weeks alongside technical work is the
intended shape. Ten sessions in one week produces ten artifacts nobody uses. Each
worksheet ends with a one-week follow-through task; start the next session by asking
who did it and what happened. Each plan's opening names the follow-through from the
session numbered before it. If you move a session, as the exceptions above suggest,
open with the follow-through from whichever session your cohort ran last.

**A short version.** If you have three slots, run Orientation, The Resilient STEM
Scholar and The Savvy Researcher. Those cover the three most expensive gaps: unstated
expectations, misread failure, and credit handled too late.

## How these relate to the module session kits

Modules 01, 02, 17, 19 and 22–25 already have [session
kits]({{ '/teaching/sessions/' | relative_url }}) on overlapping themes. The kits
teach a module's content; these workshops rehearse decisions. Where they overlap, the
workshop plan names the module and says what is different. Orientation assumes the
Module 02 navigation playbook exists or is built first, for example.

## Facilitating these safely

Read the [Facilitator Guide]({{ '/teaching/facilitator-guide/' | relative_url }})
before running any of them. Four rules apply to every session here:

- **Cases, not confessions.** Every worksheet uses an invented case. Learners may
  apply it to their own situation in private writing. Nobody is asked to disclose
  anything personal aloud.
- **State the norm, then argue with it.** Naming a norm is not endorsing it. Each
  plan leaves time to ask whether the norm is fair.
- **Know your reporting obligations.** Sessions 8 and 9 can surface harassment or
  misconduct. Check before the session whether your role makes you a required
  reporter under your institution's policy, and say so at the start.
- **Model the gap.** A facilitator narrating a norm they got wrong does more than
  the worksheet does.

Model responses are public and formative. They show one defensible answer, not the
only one. The rubrics are local teaching rubrics, not validated assessment instruments.
