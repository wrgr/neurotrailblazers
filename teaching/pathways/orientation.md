*Workshop 1 of the [Professional Pathways workshops]({{ '/teaching/pathways/' | relative_url }}).
90 minutes, no slides required.*

- [Learner worksheet]({{ '/teaching/pathways/orientation-activity/' | relative_url }})
- [Instructor model responses]({{ '/teaching/pathways/orientation-answers/' | relative_url }})
- Background: [Lab norms]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/lab-norms/' | relative_url }}), especially
  *What a PI expects but will not say*

**Question:** What is expected of me here, and how do I find out what is not written down?

## Outcomes and preparation

Learners distinguish stated from enforced expectations; convert an ambiguous
instruction into a question with a deliverable and a date; write their own draft
expectations list; and prepare the week-four check-in that tests it against their
supervisor's view.

[Module 02]({{ '/modules/module02/' | relative_url }}) builds a lab navigation
playbook: norms inventory, scripts, a support map and an ethics commitment. This
workshop does something narrower. It takes one relationship, the supervisor, and
turns guesses about it into a list the supervisor can correct. Run Module 02 first
if you can. If not, this session stands alone.

Before class, read the lab norms page and the worksheet case. Print the worksheet or
share it as a document. Learners need nothing else. **The case is invented.** Nobody
is asked to describe their own supervisor aloud.

## Timed plan and facilitator cues

- **0–10 min:** open with your own example. Name one thing a supervisor expected of you
  that nobody said, and how you found out. Keep it specific and a little embarrassing.
  Ask: "How would you have found that out sooner?" Collect answers on the board.
- **10–20 min:** stated versus enforced. Read three items from *What a PI expects but
  will not say* aloud: "have a look at this" carries a deliverable, silence is not
  consent, and bad news arrives early. Ask which ones learners have already met.
  Point out that labs rarely write any of the three down.
- **20–45 min:** pairs complete worksheet sections 1 and 2 on the invented case. At
  minute 35, check that pairs are separating what Dana was *told* from what Dana
  *inferred*. Most first drafts merge them.
- **45–55 min:** debrief section 2 as a whole group. Ask one pair to read their
  rewritten instruction. Test it: "Could the supervisor answer that in one sentence?"
  If not, the question is still too open.
- **55–75 min:** individuals complete section 3, their own expectations list and
  check-in script. This is private writing. Circulate, but do not read over shoulders
  uninvited.
- **75–85 min:** pairs swap *only the script*, not the list, and give one piece of
  feedback: is it a question the supervisor can correct, or a request for reassurance?
- **85–90 min:** exit ticket. Name the follow-through task and the date you will ask
  about it.

## Facilitator cautions

Do not present the norms as universal. Labs differ, and the right stuck-threshold in
one group is wrong in the next. That is exactly why the session teaches learners to
ask, not to memorize.

Expect one learner to say their supervisor is unreachable or hostile. Do not solve
that in the room. Acknowledge that the check-in assumes a functioning relationship and
point to the [Conflict]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/conflict/' | relative_url }}) page and
to a named person in your program they can talk to afterward.

Learners from families without academic experience may be doing this for the first
time. Learners arriving from industry may over-trust their existing workplace norms.
The case lets both groups practice without revealing which group they are in.

## Assessment and follow-through

Use the four-dimension rubric on the model responses page. Collect the worksheet
sections 1 and 2; section 3 stays with the learner unless they choose to share it.

**Follow-through:** hold the week-four conversation (or its equivalent, if the
learner is further along) before the next workshop, then add one correction from the
supervisor to the list. Start [The Resilient STEM
Scholar]({{ '/teaching/pathways/resilient-scholar/' | relative_url }}) by asking who
did it and what changed.
