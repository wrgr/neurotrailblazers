**Time:** 20 minutes in pairs, 15 minutes in pairs, then 15 minutes alone. The case
below is **invented**. Sam, Priya and Dr. Halvorsen are not real people.

## The case

Sam is a second-year student in a connectomics lab. On the 3rd, Sam showed a result
at lab meeting: reciprocal connections between two cell types were enriched 1.8-fold
over a random model, across 140 proofread cells. Dr. Halvorsen, the PI, was pleased.
Here is Sam's next week:

1. **Monday.** Sam reruns the analysis against the newest materialization version.
   The enrichment drops to 1.2-fold. 22 of the 140 cells now resolve to different
   objects. Sam's notebook does not say which version the original number came from.
2. **Tuesday.** A quality review flags 9 of Sam's 60 recent proofreading edits as
   wrong. All 9 are merges across thin axon bridges.
3. **Wednesday.** An abstract Sam drafted with Priya, a postdoc, is rejected. One
   review says: "The enrichment claim is stated without a null model."
4. **Thursday.** Sam skips lab meeting and sends no message. At coffee, Priya
   mentions that the new version fixed a large merge in the region Sam studies.
5. **Friday.** Sam writes in the notebook: "I am not good enough for this lab."

Nobody has asked Sam anything yet. Dr. Halvorsen still thinks the 1.8-fold result stands.

## 1. What is each failure evidence about? (20 minutes)

For each of the five events, write three things:

- **Evidence about:** the specific thing it tells you something about. Name a thing:
  a data version, an error class, a claim, a choice.
- **Not evidence about:** what it does not show, even though it may feel as if it does.
- **Next check:** one action that would tell Sam more, doable in under a week.

Then answer two questions:

- Which event is the only one about a choice Sam made?
- Which event, if the next check confirms it, means the *project* may need to change
  rather than Sam's method?

## 2. The bad-week report (15 minutes)

Write the message Sam should send Dr. Halvorsen on Friday. Keep it under 150 words.
It should:

- lead with the fact the supervisor most needs, with a number;
- say what is affected, and what is not;
- say what Sam has already done;
- offer a recommendation with a default and a date;
- apologize once, briefly, at the end, if at all.

Use the four-part mistake script on the [Lab norms]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/lab-norms/' | relative_url }})
page as a model: what happened, what is affected, what you have done, what you propose.

## 3. One verdict of your own (15 minutes, private)

This section is yours. You do not have to share it.

1. Pick one recent setback in your own work. It can be small.
2. Write the sentence you said to yourself about it. Copy it exactly, even if it is harsh.
3. Rewrite it as a diagnosis: what the setback is evidence about, and what it is not.
4. Can you name the specific thing you did not know or did not do? If yes, write it
   and the next check. If no, write the question you would ask someone to find out.
5. Write a project trigger: "If ___ happens by ___, I will raise with ___ whether the
   question itself needs to change."
6. Name one person you will tell about the setback this week, and what you will ask them.

If a verdict you wrote does not move when you name the gap, or it follows you home
most days, that is worth taking to someone qualified. The facilitator will name where.

## Peer check and exit ticket

Swap Sam's report with your partner, not your private page. Your partner answers one
question: *could Dr. Halvorsen reply with yes or no?* If not, revise the last sentence.

Exit ticket: "The next check I will run on my own setback is ___. I will run it by ___ (date)."

**Follow-through this week:** add an EXPECTED line to every notebook entry for one
week. Send one status report in the section 2 format on real work. Bring the reply
to the next workshop.

**Success criteria:** each event names what it is evidence about, not how Sam feels;
project and process are told apart; the report leads with a number and ends with a
dated default; your private rewrite replaces a verdict with a check.
