These responses use the worksheet's **invented** case. They are examples, not the only
acceptable wording. This page is public and suitable for formative feedback.

## 1. What each failure is evidence about

| Event | Evidence about | Not evidence about | Next check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enrichment drops to 1.2-fold | The data version. The 1.8-fold number was computed on an unpinned version with a known merge. | Sam's ability to do the analysis. | Rerun on cells whose IDs are stable across both versions; see whether the drop sits in the fixed region. |
| 9 of 60 edits flagged | One error class: merges across thin bridges. A skill gap, named and closable. | Sam's proofreading in general. 51 edits passed. | Ask the reviewer for two examples; re-check Sam's other edits of that type. |
| Abstract rejected | The claim. It was stated without a null. | Whether Sam belongs in the field. | Write the null in words, as in [Technical practice]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/technical-practice/' | relative_url }}) norm 21. |
| Skipped lab meeting, no message | A choice Sam made. | Anything about the science. | Send a note now; it costs one line. |
| "I am not good enough" | Nothing specific. It is a verdict, not an observation. | Any of the four events above. | Apply the test on the [Belonging]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/belonging/' | relative_url }}) page: name the specific thing. |

**The only event about Sam's own choice** is Thursday. It is also the most expensive.
Silence is read as progress, so Dr. Halvorsen still believes a number Sam now doubts.

**The possible project signal** is Monday. If the stable-ID rerun also shows a small
effect, the question "are these pairs enriched?" may have a smaller or null answer.
That is a conversation about the project, held with the supervisor. It is a result,
not a failure of Sam's.

Accept the answer that Tuesday also reflects a process gap: no EXPECTED lines and no
pinned version meant nothing caught either problem early. That is a protocol fix, as
the lab norms page says, not a call for more care.

## 2. Model bad-week report

> "Three things, most important first. The 1.8-fold enrichment I showed on the 3rd was
> computed on an older materialization version. On the current version it is 1.2-fold,
> and 22 of the 140 cells changed IDs, mostly in the region where the large merge was
> fixed. So I think the result I presented is overstated. Second, quality review flagged
> 9 of my 60 edits, all thin-bridge merges; I am re-checking my other edits of that type
> by Wednesday. Third, the abstract was rejected for lacking a null. My proposal: rerun
> on stable-ID cells at both versions with a stated null, and bring both numbers to lab
> meeting on the 17th. Unless you would rather talk first, I will start Monday. Sorry
> for missing Thursday without a note."

It leads with the fact the supervisor is currently wrong about. It gives numbers,
names what is affected, and ends with a default the PI can approve or stop. The single
apology is last, so nobody has to manage Sam's feelings before acting.

## 3. Private verdict: what good looks like

The private page is not collected. Learners who choose to share it tend to fall into
two patterns worth naming aloud, without naming the learner.

**Strong:** the harsh sentence is copied exactly, then replaced by something checkable
("I did not state a null; I will write it in words and ask a labmate to read it by
Friday").
The project trigger has a date and a person. The named confidant is asked a question,
not for comfort.

**Weak:** the rewrite is a kinder verdict ("I am doing my best") rather than a
diagnosis. It feels better and changes nothing. Or the setback is described only as a
feeling, with no event attached. Ask: "What happened, on what day, and what was the
number?"

## Feedback rubric

Score four dimensions **0–2 each**:

1. **Evidence separated from verdict.** 2 = every row names a specific thing in the
   middle column; 1 = most rows; 0 = rows describe feelings or character.
2. **Project and process told apart.** 2 = Monday is flagged as a possible project
   signal and Thursday as Sam's choice; 1 = one of the two; 0 = neither.
3. **Report leads with facts.** 2 = the first sentence carries the overstated result
   with a number; 1 = the fact is present but buried; 0 = the result is omitted.
4. **Report ends in a decision.** 2 = recommendation, default and date; 1 = two of the
   three; 0 = the report ends in apology or a question with no proposal.

A proficient response scores at least 6/8 with no zero on dimension 1. This is a local
teaching rubric, not a validated assessment instrument.

Do not grade how the learner feels about failure, how resilient they seem, or whether
their own setback was "serious enough". None of those is observable from a worksheet,
and grading them would teach learners to perform calm.
