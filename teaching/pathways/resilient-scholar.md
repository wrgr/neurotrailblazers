*Workshop 2 of the [Professional Pathways workshops]({{ '/teaching/pathways/' | relative_url }}).
90 minutes, no slides required.*

- [Learner worksheet]({{ '/teaching/pathways/resilient-scholar-activity/' | relative_url }})
- [Instructor model responses]({{ '/teaching/pathways/resilient-scholar-answers/' | relative_url }})
- Background: [Belonging]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/belonging/' | relative_url }}), especially
  *The distinction that does all the work*; [Lab norms]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/lab-norms/' | relative_url }}),
  especially *When you have made a mistake that affects someone else's work*

**Question:** What do I do when the work fails, and how do I tell a failing project from a failing me?

## Outcomes and preparation

Learners sort a run of setbacks by what each one is evidence about: the data, the
question, a method, a skill, or a choice. They separate a verdict ("I am not good
enough") from a diagnosis ("9 of 60 edits were wrong, all of one kind"). They write a
bad-week report that carries the facts first and ends in a decision. In private, they
rewrite one verdict of their own as a checkable claim with a next step.

[Module 02]({{ '/modules/module02/' | relative_url }}) states *failure as data* as a
principle, and [Module 01]({{ '/modules/module01/' | relative_url }}) names the
motivation gap. Neither asks learners to diagnose a specific failure or to write the
message that reports it. This workshop does both. It works best after Orientation,
because the bad-week report uses the same habit as the check-in: say it early and
make it correctable.

Before class, read the belonging page's *The test* and the lab norms four-part
mistake script. Print the worksheet. **The case is invented.** Nobody is asked to
describe a failure of their own aloud.

## Timed plan and facilitator cues

- **0–10 min:** follow-through from Orientation: ask who held the check-in and what
  one correction they added to their list. Take two answers, no more. Then narrate one
  failure of your own. Say what you concluded about yourself
  at the time, and what it turned out to be evidence about. Ask: "What would have
  told you sooner that it was the data, not you?"
- **10–20 min:** the distinction. Write three columns on the board: *what happened*,
  *what it is evidence about*, *what it is not evidence about*. Read the belonging
  page's line about treating a rejection as a verdict rather than as information about
  one piece of work. Ask who has done that this term. Hands are enough.
- **20–40 min:** pairs complete worksheet section 1 on the invented case. At minute
  30, check that the middle column names a thing (a version, an error class, a claim)
  and not a feeling. Most first drafts put "Sam is struggling" there.
- **40–50 min:** debrief section 1. Ask which event is the only one about Sam's own
  choices. Expected answer: skipping lab meeting without a word. Ask which event might
  mean the *project* has to change. Expected answer: the shrinking effect.
- **50–65 min:** pairs complete section 2, Sam's bad-week report.
- **65–80 min:** individuals complete section 3. This is private writing. Circulate,
  but do not read over shoulders uninvited.
- **80–87 min:** pairs swap Sam's report, not their private page. The partner checks
  one thing: does the report end in a decision the supervisor can approve or refuse?
- **87–90 min:** exit ticket. Name the follow-through task and the date you will ask.

## Facilitator cautions

This is not counseling, and the plan should not drift into it. The workshop teaches a
diagnostic habit for setbacks in the work. Persistent low mood, anxiety, or distress
that follows someone home is outside its scope. Say at the start where your
institution's counseling or student support service is, by name, and repeat it at the
end. Check before the session what your role obliges you to report if a learner
discloses harm.

Do not tell the room that every failure is about the project. Some are about skill,
and a skill gap named precisely is good news because it closes. Some are about the
environment. If a learner describes being treated differently from peers, do not
resolve it in the room. Point to the belonging page's *When the answer is that the
environment is bad*, to [Conflict]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/conflict/' | relative_url }}),
and to a named person they can talk to afterwards.

Grit language can move responsibility for a bad environment onto the person in it.
The belonging page says so directly. Hold the same line here: resilience means
reading evidence accurately, not enduring anything.

## Assessment and follow-through

Use the four-dimension rubric on the model responses page. Collect sections 1 and 2;
section 3 stays with the learner unless they choose to share it.

**Follow-through:** for one week, add an EXPECTED line to every notebook entry, as in
[Meta-learning]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/meta-learning/' | relative_url }}) §6. Send
one status report in the section 2 format on real work, good week or bad. Start
[Charting Your Course in
Research]({{ '/teaching/pathways/charting-your-course/' | relative_url }}) by asking
who sent it and what came back.
