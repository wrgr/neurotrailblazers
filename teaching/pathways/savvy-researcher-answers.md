## 1. The machine, mapped

| Thread | Strand | Who decides | Evidence | By when | Tomás asks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Stipend | Funding | Funder on the renewal; department on what "five years" covers | Review of the renewal; the program's funding policy | Award end date | Graduate administrator: "What does my five-year offer cover if an award lapses?" Dr. Brennan: which award, end date, renewal timing |
| 2 Idris draft | Credit | First and last author, usually together | Contribution, against whatever criteria the lab uses | Before submission | Dr. Idris, directly and privately |
| 3 Lindqvist | Credit and scope | Nobody yet | None yet | Before any code runs | Dr. Brennan first, then Dr. Lindqvist: the memo |
| 4 Revision | Review | The editor | The response letter, weighed against the editor's sentence | Journal's revision deadline | Dr. Brennan: is the new analysis large enough to change the author list? |
| 5 Review | Review and credit | The editor, on who may see a manuscript | The journal's reviewer policy | Before Tuesday | Dr. Brennan: "Could I be named to the editor as co-reviewer?" |

**The map** has Tomás in the middle and at least these nodes: Dr. Brennan, Dr. Idris,
Dr. Lindqvist, two editors (thread 4 and thread 5), the funder, the institution holding
the award, and the department that made the offer. Money flows funder → institution →
budget line → stipend. Decisions on the revision flow editor → authors. Credit arrows
are dashed because none has been decided.

**The thread to ask about now** is thread 3. Nothing has started, so the memo is cheap.
Accept thread 2 if the learner argues it has a nearer deadline; it does, but the draft
exists, so the conversation is repair rather than planning. Many pairs miss thread 5.
Career mechanics is direct: do not ghost-write a review, ask to be named. Many journals
also require reviewers to tell the editor, or ask permission, before involving anyone
else. Tomás can check the journal's reviewer policy.

Thread 1 is not a crisis. A multi-year offer is often a department commitment that may be
paid from different sources in different years. It is still a guess until someone says it.

## 2. Memo and scripts

**Memo for thread 3:**

1. Question: synapse QC on the Lindqvist volume, using Tomás's script.
2. Tomás provides the script, one run and a short QC report; the Lindqvist lab provides
   volume access and a contact who knows its segmentation.
3. Data stay on the Lindqvist lab's systems; Tomás may show the QC method, not their data,
   in lab meeting.
4. Tomás runs and reports; the Lindqvist lab interprets and writes.
5. As currently understood: acknowledgement for a single run; middle authorship if the
   script is adapted or the QC appears as a figure. Revisit if scope grows.
6. If it stops, the script stays with the Brennan lab and the data with the Lindqvist lab;
   any QC report already sent can be used with acknowledgement.
7. Either side may publish their own part; neither publishes the other's without asking.

**Proposing it:** "Happy to help Dr. Lindqvist. Before I start, could we write half a page
on what I'm delivering and where it sits for credit as we currently see it? Then if it
grows, we'll both notice."

**CRediT for thread 2:** Software (the QC script), Validation (the QC checks the lab runs),
and Investigation or Data curation for the proofreading. Projects classify proofreading
differently. CRediT describes contributions. It does not decide whether they meet
authorship criteria; the lab or journal does.

**Message to Dr. Idris:** "Thanks for sending the draft. I saw it uses my 900 proofread
segments and the QC script. I think I'm in the acknowledgements; could we talk about
whether that's the right place? I may have missed a conversation." It assumes an oversight,
names the work, and leaves room for a correct answer of "acknowledgement".

## 3. Personal map: what good looks like

The private section is not collected. Learners who share it tend to show two patterns.

**Strong:** at least one item marked **guessed**, one project marked **assumed** or
**unknown**, and a question with a noun and a date in it ("Which award is my position on,
and when does it end?").

**Weak:** everything marked **known** or **agreed**, or a question like "Am I going to be
OK for funding?" That asks for reassurance. It will get "don't worry", which career
mechanics notes is sincere and is not information you can plan against.

## Feedback rubric

Score four dimensions **0–2 each**:

1. **Deciders named.** 2 = every thread has a plausible decider and at least two are not
   the PI; 1 = some; 0 = the PI for everything.
2. **Map is a system.** 2 = funder, institution and editor appear, with labeled arrows;
   1 = people only; 0 = a list, not a map.
3. **Memo is usable.** 2 = all seven lines, Tomás's delivery first, stop condition stated;
   1 = five or six lines; 0 = no authorship line.
4. **Scripts assume good faith and name the work.** 2 = both scripts do; 1 = one does;
   0 = either reads as an accusation.

A proficient response scores at least 6/8 with no zero on dimension 3. This is a local
teaching rubric, not a validated assessment instrument.

Do not grade the authorship outcome a learner proposes. Acknowledgement can be the right
answer. Grade whether the question is asked early, specifically and in writing.
