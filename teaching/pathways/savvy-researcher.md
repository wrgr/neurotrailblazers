*Workshop 7 of the [Professional Pathways workshops]({{ '/teaching/pathways/' | relative_url }}).
90 minutes, no slides required.*

- [Learner worksheet]({{ '/teaching/pathways/savvy-researcher-activity/' | relative_url }})
- [Instructor model responses]({{ '/teaching/pathways/savvy-researcher-answers/' | relative_url }})
- Background: [Career mechanics]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/career-mechanics/' | relative_url }}),
  sections 1, 2 and 5, and the *Authorship and credit* section of
  [Lab norms]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/lab-norms/' | relative_url }}#authorship-and-credit)

**Question:** How does the system actually work — funding, authorship, review, credit?

## Outcomes and preparation

Learners name who decides, on what evidence and on what timetable, for funding, review
and credit; draw that as a map for one invented student; write a half-page authorship and
scope memo before work starts; and find the one question about their own position they
have been guessing the answer to.

[Career mechanics]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/career-mechanics/' | relative_url }}) explains
the machine as reading. [Module 19]({{ '/modules/module19/' | relative_url }}) sets
authorship rules for a consortium and teaches how to write a review. [Module
02]({{ '/modules/module02/' | relative_url }}) includes the uncredited-annotation scenario.
This workshop does something narrower: it puts five ordinary requests on one student's
desk in one week and asks learners to see that each is a funding, review or credit
decision, and who makes it. The memo is the career mechanics seven-line memo, written for
a real-looking request rather than described.

Before class, read career mechanics sections 1, 2 and 5 and the lab norms authorship
section. Print the worksheet. Have the [CRediT taxonomy](https://credit.niso.org/) open
to show its fourteen roles. **The case is invented.** Nobody is asked to describe their
own funding or authorship situation aloud.

## Timed plan and facilitator cues

- **0–8 min:** follow-through from workshop 6: ask who had a senior person put a hard
  question to them and what they were not expecting. Then tell one story of credit or
  funding you found out about too late. Ask: "When could you have asked, and whom?"
- **8–20 min:** the machine on the board, three strands. Funding: an award goes to an
  institution, a PI is responsible, budget lines have end dates. Review: the editor
  decides; reviews advise. Credit: author positions, CRediT roles, and criteria of the
  ICMJE type. Say that none of this is secret. It is undocumented.
- **20–40 min:** pairs complete section 1 on the invented case. At minute 30, check the
  "who decides" column. Most pairs write "Dr. Brennan" for everything. The editor decides
  the revision, the institution holds the award, and nobody has decided authorship yet.
- **40–48 min:** debrief section 1. Ask which of Tomás's five threads is not his to
  decide, and which is his to ask about. Expect the ghost review to be missed.
- **48–66 min:** pairs complete section 2, the memo and scripts. At minute 56, check that
  the Lindqvist memo is framed as scope, not trust, and that it states what Tomás delivers
  first.
- **66–72 min:** one pair reads their Idris script aloud. Test it: "Does it assume an
  oversight, and does it name what Tomás did?"
- **72–84 min:** section 3, private writing. Do not read over shoulders uninvited.
- **84–90 min:** peer check on the one question only, then the exit ticket.

## Facilitator cautions

Do not give numbers. Funding success rates, salaries, review times and deadlines vary by
country, agency, field and year, and career mechanics leaves them out on purpose. Send
learners to the funder's own pages and their department administrator.

Authorship order differs by field; some fields list authors alphabetically. Say so. Do
not present the case's conventions as universal.

A learner may describe an authorship dispute that is live, or a position tied to a visa.
Do not adjudicate it in the room and do not probe. Point to the
[Conflict]({{ '/hidden-curriculum/conflict/' | relative_url }}) page and a named person:
a graduate director, ombuds office or second mentor. If what surfaces sounds like research
misconduct or a breach of reviewer confidentiality, check your institutional reporting
obligations before asking for details. [Professional
Conduct]({{ '/teaching/pathways/professional-conduct/' | relative_url }}) is the session
for obligations; this one is for mechanics.

Do not cast the PI as a villain. Dr. Brennan in the case is busy, not malicious. Most
credit problems start as nobody asking.

## Assessment and follow-through

Use the four-dimension rubric on the model responses page. Collect sections 1 and 2.
Section 3 stays with the learner unless they choose to share it.

**Follow-through:** ask the one question from section 3 this week, in the words you
drafted, and write down the answer in a dated note. Start [Professional Conduct in
STEM]({{ '/teaching/pathways/professional-conduct/' | relative_url }}) by asking who asked
it and what they learned.
