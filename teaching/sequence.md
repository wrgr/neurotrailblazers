Start here if you want to teach a small connectomics block now. These **four 90-minute
sessions** use existing decks with selected slides, activities, instructor cues and
model responses. They do not replace the full nine-unit Technical Course or the
three-lecture EN.585.781 sequence.

## Session 1: Introduction to Connectomics

**Question:** What can a wiring diagram establish?

Teach the claim framework and analysis units, then have pairs write a study brief.
The output is a structural endpoint with a denominator, comparison and explicit
non-claim. Introductory neuroscience is helpful; no programming is required.

## Between sessions: 20 minutes

Read [Synapse Detection]({{ '/content-library/infrastructure/synapse-detection/' | relative_url }}),
focusing on localization, partner assignment and evaluation units. Bring the study
brief and underline the measurement most vulnerable to a missed or misassigned
synapse. No account or dataset download is needed.

## Session 2: Synapse Detection

**Question:** What errors could change that structural result?

Learners calculate metrics on explicitly synthetic data and write a validation plan.
They then revise their Session 1 brief to name the relevant error, provenance and
additional evidence needed. The arithmetic exercise requires only a calculator.

- [Timed plan, instructor cues and slides]({{ '/teaching/lectures/synapse-detection/' | relative_url }})

## Session 3: Tools and Methods

**Question:** Can another person reproduce the number in your figure?

Bring the revised study brief and audit from Session 2. Learners trace the relevant
pipeline artifacts, compare two synthetic snapshots, write a recoverable methods
record and estimate raw storage. No live data or accounts are required. Python 3
is optional; the same query can be completed from the worksheet's printed rows.

## Session 4: Algorithms and Applications

**Question:** Does the structural result exceed what the chosen null explains?

Carry forward the study brief, audit and methods record. Learners build a tiny
directed graph, compare reciprocity with an exactly enumerated null, test a threshold
and an uncertain edge, then write an analysis card. Python 3 is optional; all inputs
and the exact census are supplied on the worksheet. No accounts are needed.

## Preparation and pacing

Open the selected decks before teaching. Keep the timed plan in a second tab or print it.
Slide numbers include the cover. Share only the worksheet during the activity;
answer keys are public and intended for formative learning, not secure exams.
Embedded notes remain in the editable Marp sources, with delivery cues on the plans.

For a **half-day workshop**, run the first two sessions with a 15-minute break: 195 minutes
of room time. Replace the between-session reading with a short advance reading.
For separate meetings, allow the 20-minute reading between them. For a **full
Introduction lecture**, use its existing three-part, approximately 150-minute route
instead of the selected-slide session and schedule breaks separately.

For the **first three sessions**, allow 270 minutes of teaching plus two 15-minute
breaks: five hours, excluding lunch. For **all four**, allow 360 minutes of teaching
plus three 15-minute breaks: **6 hours 45 minutes**, excluding lunch. Two half-days
are another option. The Session 1-to-2 reading remains advance work for a single-day
event. Sessions 3 and 4 require no additional homework beyond bringing prior artifacts.

If discussion runs long, omit optional history slides before cutting the activity
or its debrief. The learning evidence is the revised claim, not the number of slides shown.

## What comes next

Use the final analysis card to choose a focused extension: a real dataset query,
a proofreading exercise or an applications seminar. For hands-on depth, choose a
[module session kit]({{ '/teaching/sessions/' | relative_url }}) after checking its
prerequisites. These four packs are complete formative sessions;
semester-length syllabus maps and model answers for all 25 modules remain separate work.
